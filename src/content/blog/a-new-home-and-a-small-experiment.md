If you're reading this, you're on my new website.

For a while my personal site lived on Wix. It served its purpose — but I'd been wanting more control, faster pages, cleaner code, and honestly a lower recurring cost. So I've spend two weekends ago to move it.

The site you're on now is built with [Astro](https://astro.build), hosted on Cloudflare Pages, and deployed from a Git repository I own. Everything is static, fast, and mine.

## The small experiment

Here's the part I want to be open about: I built this whole thing with **[Claude](https://claude.ai) by Anthropic** as my collaborator. The design direction, the code, the deployment setup, even the editing of the first post — done in conversation with an AI assistant, over a series of sessions.

I wanted to see what it's like to actually ship a real project with an LLM in the loop. Not a toy, not a demo — my personal website, published to my own domain, with my name on it. Two goals:

1. **Understand these tools deeply.** As someone leading engineering teams, I need a first-hand feel for how AI collaboration actually works in practice — the good, the awkward, and the surprising.
2. **Save some money.** No Wix subscription. Cloudflare Pages is free. The only cost now is my `.sg` domain renewal.

I'll write more about the experience in a future post — what worked well, what didn't, and what a senior executive who still codes should take away from working this way.

## And the real point

The site is also a kickoff.

My [first post](/post/twenty-five-years-since-frankston) is a reflection on going back to my university campus twenty-five years after I graduated. Standing there, I left with a renewed sense of optimism — that whatever's next is more opportunity than obstacle.

It felt right to launch this new place with that piece.

Have a look around. And if you're an aspiring technologist reading this, especially early in your career — reach out. I'd genuinely like to hear from you.
